Iron deficiency anemia is a microcytic, hypochromic anemia characterized by low serum ferritin (a marker of depleted iron stores) and elevated total iron-binding capacity (reflecting increased transferrin production in the setting of iron deficiency). In adults, especially men and postmenopausal women, the most common underlying cause is chronic blood loss, frequently from the gastrointestinal tract, and evaluation often includes investigation for a bleeding source. This distinguishes iron deficiency anemia from other microcytic anemias like thalassemia trait, which typically has normal or low-normal RBC indices proportionate to the degree of anemia and normal iron studies, and from anemia of chronic inflammation, which usually shows low or normal TIBC with elevated ferritin due to a functional iron block rather than true iron store depletion. Vitamin B12 deficiency causes a macrocytic, not microcytic, anemia.
