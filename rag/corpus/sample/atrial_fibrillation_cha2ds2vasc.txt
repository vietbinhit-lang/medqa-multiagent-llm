In patients with atrial fibrillation, the decision of whether to start long-term anticoagulation to prevent stroke is guided by the CHA2DS2-VASc score, which assigns points for risk factors including congestive heart failure, hypertension, age, diabetes, prior stroke or thromboembolism, vascular disease, and sex category. A higher score indicates a higher annual risk of stroke and generally supports anticoagulation, most commonly with a direct oral anticoagulant or warfarin, unless contraindicated. This tool is distinct from other clinical scoring systems used in different contexts, such as the Child-Pugh score (used to assess severity of liver cirrhosis), the Glasgow Coma Scale (used to assess level of consciousness after brain injury), and the APACHE II score (used to estimate mortality risk in critically ill ICU patients).
