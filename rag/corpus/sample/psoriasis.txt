Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated skin condition that classically presents as well-demarcated, erythematous plaques covered with a silvery-white scale, commonly on extensor surfaces such as the elbows and knees, as well as the scalp and lower back. Nail changes, including pitting, onycholysis, and oil-drop discoloration, are common associated findings and can help distinguish psoriasis from other papulosquamous conditions. This clinical picture differs from atopic dermatitis (which favors flexural surfaces and is intensely pruritic with a different morphology), tinea corporis (a fungal infection with an annular, scaling border and possible response to antifungal treatment), and contact dermatitis (which follows an exposure pattern and lacks the characteristic silvery scale). Treatment ranges from topical corticosteroids and vitamin D analogs for limited disease to phototherapy or systemic/biologic agents for more extensive or refractory disease.
