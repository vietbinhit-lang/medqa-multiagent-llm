In a patient with sudden focal neurologic deficits (such as unilateral weakness and slurred speech) suggestive of acute ischemic stroke, the most important immediate diagnostic step before any consideration of thrombolytic therapy is a non-contrast CT scan of the head. This step is essential to rapidly distinguish ischemic stroke from hemorrhagic stroke, because thrombolytics (such as tPA) are beneficial in ischemic stroke but can be catastrophic if given in the setting of intracranial hemorrhage. Time is critical, since thrombolytic therapy is only effective within a limited window from symptom onset. Lumbar puncture is not part of the initial stroke workup and is reserved for suspected subarachnoid hemorrhage with a negative CT, or suspected CNS infection. MRI and carotid ultrasound provide additional information but should not delay the initial non-contrast CT and rapid triage for reperfusion therapy.
