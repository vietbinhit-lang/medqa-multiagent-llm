Gout is a crystal-induced inflammatory arthritis caused by deposition of monosodium urate crystals, classically presenting with acute, severe pain, swelling, and erythema of a single joint, most often the first metatarsophalangeal joint (podagra). Arthrocentesis with polarized light microscopy is the diagnostic gold standard: gout shows needle-shaped crystals that are negatively birefringent, whereas pseudogout (calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease) shows rhomboid crystals that are positively birefringent. Septic arthritis must always be considered and excluded with synovial fluid Gram stain and culture, since it can mimic gout clinically but requires urgent antibiotic therapy rather than anti-inflammatory treatment alone. Acute gout flares are typically treated with NSAIDs, colchicine, or corticosteroids, while urate-lowering therapy is used for long-term prevention rather than acute flare management.
