Uncomplicated cystitis is a lower urinary tract infection in an otherwise healthy, non-pregnant patient, presenting with dysuria, urinary frequency and urgency, and suprapubic discomfort, typically without fever or flank pain (which would suggest upper tract involvement such as pyelonephritis). Urinalysis often shows pyuria and bacteriuria. First-line oral antibiotic choices for uncomplicated cystitis in many guidelines include nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (where local resistance rates allow), and fosfomycin. Fluoroquinolones such as ciprofloxacin are effective but are generally reserved as alternative or second-line agents rather than first-line therapy for uncomplicated cystitis, given concerns about resistance and side effects. Vancomycin and clindamycin are not standard therapy for typical uncomplicated urinary tract infections, as they do not reliably cover the usual gram-negative uropathogens such as Escherichia coli.
