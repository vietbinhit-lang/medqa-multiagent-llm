Warfarin is an oral anticoagulant that works by inhibiting vitamin K epoxide reductase, an enzyme required to recycle vitamin K into its active form. Because vitamin K is a necessary cofactor for the hepatic synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X (as well as the natural anticoagulant proteins C and S), inhibiting this pathway reduces the production of functional versions of these factors, producing an anticoagulant effect over several days as existing clotting factors are cleared from circulation. This mechanism differs from direct oral anticoagulants: dabigatran directly inhibits thrombin (factor IIa), while agents such as apixaban and rivaroxaban directly inhibit factor Xa. Because of its delayed onset and interaction with diet and other medications, warfarin therapy requires monitoring with the INR (international normalized ratio) to maintain a therapeutic anticoagulation range.
