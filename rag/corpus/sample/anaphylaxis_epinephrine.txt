Anaphylaxis is a severe, potentially life-threatening systemic allergic reaction that can present with urticaria (hives), angioedema (such as lip or facial swelling), bronchospasm with wheezing, and hypotension, often triggered by an exposure such as an insect sting, food, or medication. The most important immediate treatment is intramuscular epinephrine, typically injected into the anterolateral thigh, because it rapidly reverses the airway, breathing, and circulatory manifestations through its combined alpha- and beta-adrenergic effects. Antihistamines such as diphenhydramine and inhaled beta-agonists like albuterol may be used as adjuncts for cutaneous symptoms or bronchospasm, and corticosteroids may help prevent a biphasic reaction, but none of these should be given as the primary or first treatment in place of epinephrine, since delaying epinephrine is associated with worse outcomes.
