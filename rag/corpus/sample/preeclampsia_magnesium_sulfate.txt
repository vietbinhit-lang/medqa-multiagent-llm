Preeclampsia is defined by new-onset hypertension after 20 weeks' gestation accompanied by proteinuria or other signs of end-organ involvement. Severe features include markedly elevated blood pressure (such as greater than or equal to 160/110 mmHg), severe headache, visual disturbances, or laboratory abnormalities. In severe preeclampsia, intravenous magnesium sulfate is the standard of care for seizure (eclampsia) prophylaxis, because it has been shown to reduce the risk of progression to eclamptic seizures more effectively than other anticonvulsants such as phenytoin or benzodiazepines. Antihypertensive agents like intravenous labetalol are used to control severe blood pressure but do not substitute for magnesium sulfate's seizure-prevention role. Definitive treatment of preeclampsia is delivery of the fetus and placenta, timed according to gestational age and maternal-fetal status.
