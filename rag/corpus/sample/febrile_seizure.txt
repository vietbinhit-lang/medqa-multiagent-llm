A simple febrile seizure occurs in a young child (typically between 6 months and 5 years) during a febrile illness, is generalized, lasts less than 15 minutes, and does not recur within 24 hours, with a prompt return to baseline mental status and a normal neurologic exam afterward. Simple febrile seizures are generally benign, and the mainstay of management after a typical episode is reassurance of the caregivers, treatment of the underlying fever source if indicated, and outpatient follow-up, without the need for daily antiepileptic medication, emergency lumbar puncture, or head CT in a child with a normal exam and a straightforward presentation. Lumbar puncture or neuroimaging is reserved for atypical (complex) febrile seizures or when there are signs concerning for CNS infection or another underlying neurologic process.
