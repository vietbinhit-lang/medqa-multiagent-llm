Acute ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) presents with sudden severe substernal chest pain, often with radiation to the left arm or jaw, diaphoresis, and nausea. The diagnostic hallmark is ST-segment elevation on ECG in a coronary artery distribution (for example, leads II, III, and aVF for an inferior infarction). Initial management priorities, sometimes summarized as "MONA-B" in older teaching, include chewable non-enteric-coated aspirin (162-325 mg) given immediately to all patients without a contraindication, since early antiplatelet therapy reduces mortality. Oxygen is reserved for hypoxemic patients. Nitrates and morphine may be used for ongoing pain if blood pressure allows, but they do not replace reperfusion therapy. The definitive treatment is timely reperfusion, either primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) if available within the guideline-recommended window, or fibrinolytic therapy if PCI is not accessible in time. Beta-blockers are typically started once the patient is hemodynamically stable rather than as the very first intervention.
