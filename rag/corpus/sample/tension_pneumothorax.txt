Tension pneumothorax is a life-threatening emergency, often following trauma, in which air enters the pleural space and cannot escape, progressively increasing intrathoracic pressure. Classic findings include sudden severe dyspnea, absent breath sounds on the affected side, hypotension from impaired venous return, and tracheal deviation away from the affected side. Because this is a clinical diagnosis and the patient is often hemodynamically unstable, treatment should not be delayed for confirmatory chest X-ray. The most appropriate immediate management is needle decompression (needle thoracostomy), typically in the second intercostal space at the midclavicular line, to rapidly relieve the pressure, followed by definitive chest tube placement. Endotracheal intubation alone does not relieve the underlying tension physiology, and simple observation with supplemental oxygen is inadequate and dangerous in this emergency.
