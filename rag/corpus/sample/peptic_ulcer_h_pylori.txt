Helicobacter pylori is a major cause of peptic ulcer disease, including duodenal ulcers that classically improve with meals and worsen at night. Once H. pylori infection is confirmed (for example by biopsy, urea breath test, or stool antigen test), guideline-recommended treatment is eradication therapy, most commonly a proton pump inhibitor combined with two antibiotics (classic triple therapy, such as a PPI plus clarithromycin and amoxicillin, or metronidazole in penicillin-allergic patients) for 10-14 days. Proton pump inhibitor monotherapy heals the ulcer temporarily but does not eradicate the underlying infection, leading to high relapse rates. H2 receptor blockers and antacids provide symptomatic relief only and are not adequate definitive therapy when H. pylori infection has been confirmed. Confirming eradication with a follow-up test is recommended in many cases, particularly for complicated ulcers.
