Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in a previously healthy outpatient typically presents with fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and a new infiltrate on chest imaging. For outpatients without significant comorbidities, empiric antibiotic therapy is generally directed at the most common pathogens, particularly Streptococcus pneumoniae and atypical organisms such as Mycoplasma pneumoniae and Chlamydophila pneumoniae. A macrolide such as azithromycin, or doxycycline, is a standard first-line empiric choice for healthy outpatients in many guidelines. Broader-spectrum agents such as piperacillin-tazobactam or vancomycin are reserved for more severe presentations, hospitalized patients with risk factors for resistant organisms, or healthcare-associated infections, and are not first-line for a low-risk outpatient. Clinical severity scores (such as CURB-65) help decide between outpatient management, hospital admission, and ICU-level care.
